# 03 · Georges Simenon — Maigret (and the romans durs)

> **Card** · 1931–1972 · 75 Maigret novels + 28 stories; 100+ *romans durs* · atmospheric procedural ·
> 3rd person limited / past · Paris and provincial France
> **Read first:** *Pietr the Latvian* (1931) or *The Saint-Fiacre Affair* (1932)
> **Popularity:** 500–700 million copies across all works (estimates); among the most adapted crime writers
> in history, with Maigret filmed in France, Britain, Italy, Japan and the USSR.
> **Voice card:** sentence 4 · variance 3 · figuration 3 · interiority 6 · dialogue 6 · description 4 ·
> violence 4 · irony 3 · fatalism 7 · procedure 5 · sex 4 · distance 5

## What the series solved

Simenon solved the problem of **atmosphere without ornament**. He wrote fast (often a novel in about
eleven days), deliberately restricted himself to a vocabulary of roughly two thousand words, and produced
books that feel less written than *remembered*. His plots are frequently implausible; readers do not
care, because the novels deliver something more valuable than a solution — a complete social and sensory
world, entered through a policeman's ordinary body.

He also solved the **anti-deductive detective**. Maigret does not hunt clues in the Holmesian sense. He
immerses himself in the victim's street, drinks in the local café, eats the local food, and waits until he
understands how a person could have come to do such a thing. His motto is to understand, not to judge.

The *romans durs* — the non-Maigret "hard novels" — take the same method into the criminal's skull, with
an even flatter tone and no consolation of resolution. They are the closest thing the canon has to a
laboratory for psychological noir.

## Syntax and prose mechanics

- **Sentence.** Short-to-medium, mostly declarative, with very little subordination. The flatness is the
  point: it refuses to instruct the reader how to feel.
- **Paragraph.** Built from **accumulated ordinary detail** — weather, food, a chair, a hand, the sound of
  a tram — rather than from argument. Meaning emerges from the accumulation, not from a thesis sentence.
- **Dialogue.** Mundane, repetitive, often awkward. Simenon's people say the wrong thing, trail off, and
  repeat themselves. This is not a failure of ear; it is a recording of social discomfort.
- **Description.** Concrete and sensory but never lush. He names the object, the temperature, the smell,
  and stops. The famous "atmosphere" is largely a by-product of restraint.
- **Figuration.** Sparse and plain. A simile is allowed if it is the kind of comparison a tired man would
  actually make.
- **Punctuation.** Simple. Few dashes, few semicolons, almost no exclamation marks.
- **Exposition.** Through movement: Maigret walks, sits, orders, waits, and the reader learns the town by
  walking it.

> Simenon described what he meant by atmosphere as "the poetic line" — trying to do with prose what poetry
> usually does. That is the ambition hidden inside the flat sentences.

## Character psychology

**Maigret** is the canon's great *normal* detective. He is happily married, eats and drinks with pleasure,
dislikes exercise, and is warmed by domestic routine. His psychology is not organised around a wound; it is
organised around **appetite and attention**. He is curious the way a good host is curious. That makes him a
useful corrective to the wounded-detective monoculture: a protagonist can be compelling without being
damaged.

His method is empathy in the literal sense — he *feels with* the suspect, sometimes to the point of
physical identification, and then has to go home and eat dinner. The moral drama is that understanding
does not prevent arrest.

**Antagonists** are ordinary people who have been compressed by circumstance: a jealous spouse, a
humiliated clerk, a provincial notable with a secret. Simenon's radical claim is that the distance between
the detective and the killer is small and mostly luck.

**Supporting characters** are rendered through habit and class: the concierge's information economy, the
café owner's calculations, the magistrate's vanity. Simenon is superb on the way institutions distribute
dignity.

## Signature devices

1. **Restricted diction.** Simple words, repeated; complexity lives in the arrangement, not the lexicon.
2. **Atmosphere as argument.** Weather and room tell you what the social relations are.
3. **The behavioural tic.** Characterise by a repeated physical habit rather than an adjective.
4. **Immersion over interrogation.** The detective learns by inhabiting, not by asking clever questions.
5. **The flat close.** End on an ordinary action (a meal, a train, a pipe) that refuses catharsis.

## What to steal / what to avoid

**Steal:** the two-thousand-word discipline; the ordinary detail that carries meaning; the detective who
likes his life.

**Avoid:** plot convenience dressed as intuition; the flat tone that becomes merely grey. Simenon's
plainness is *tuned* — the sentences vary just enough, and the details are chosen for their emotional
charge.

## Diagnostic passage (original, in the voice)

> The rain had begun at four and by six it was still falling, a thin rain that made the cobbles shine and
> did not make anyone hurry. Maigret stood in the doorway of the café with his hands in his pockets and
> watched the people at the tram stop. A woman in a brown coat was eating something from a paper bag, and
> a man with a newspaper under his arm kept looking at his watch although no tram had passed for eleven
> minutes. Maigret ordered another beer and did not drink it. He liked the street in this weather. It was
> like a room where someone had just stopped talking.

**Annotation**

- Nine sentences, almost all under 20 words, no sentence over 30. The rhythm is a walk, not a riff.
- The details are ordinary and exact (paper bag, newspaper, eleven minutes). None is symbolic; the
  accumulation produces the mood.
- Interiority is one clause of preference ("He liked the street in this weather") plus one plain simile.
  That is as inward as Maigret gets.
- The final image is about *absence of speech*, which is the book's real subject.

## Exercise

Write 300 words in which a detective waits in a provincial café for a witness who does not arrive.
Constraints: vocabulary of common words only; no sentence over 25 words; no abstract nouns except one; at
least five concrete objects; the detective must eat or drink something; nothing may be solved.
