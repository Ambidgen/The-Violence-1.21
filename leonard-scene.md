The lot was behind a fence with a sign that said NO TRESPASSING in letters somebody had painted over twice
and then given up on. Teddy Voss stood by the office door with his arms folded, watching the tow truck come
in with a silver Camry on the hook.

"Whose is that?" Voss said.

"Fellow on Seventh," the driver said. "He parked in a handicapped space and went to get his hair cut."

"In a handicapped space."

"That's what the sign said."

Voss looked at the Camry. The front bumper was held on with wire, and somebody had written WASH ME in the
dust on the rear window, and under that, in smaller letters, PLEASE.

"You call him?"

"Left a message. He'll come down here yelling, and you'll tell him the fee, and he'll say the sign was
behind a tree."

"Was it behind a tree?"

"It was behind a tree."

Voss took out a pen and wrote the time on the clipboard. He wrote slow, the way a man writes when the
writing is the only part of the job he likes.

"Put it in the back row," he said. "Not the front. Front row's for the ones that pay."

"How do you know which ones pay?"

"The ones that pay don't park in the handicapped space. They park in the fire lane and run in and get their
hair cut fast."

The driver unhooked the Camry and let it roll back on its own weight. The tires made a sound like tape
coming off a box.

"You want me to call him again?"

"No," Voss said. "He'll call. A man who writes please in the dust always calls."
